\section{Executive Summary}

EchoBreaker is an open-access, browser-based educational tool designed to combat the rising tide of epistemic polarisation by teaching algorithmic literacy to youth. While most MIL curricula focus on \emph{what} misinformation is, EchoBreaker teaches \emph{why} it spreads by allowing students to experience algorithmic echo chambers in real-time. By engaging with four interconnected modules (Feed Simulator, Echo Escape Arena, Agency Toolkit, and Impact Command Center), users learn how recommendation systems prioritize engagement over truth. Built 100\% in English for global youth accessibility, our solution ensures wide adoption with zero-installation, low-bandwidth architecture. We aim to equip students with the cognitive framework needed to critically evaluate and diversify their social media feeds, creating a more informed and resilient digital generation.

\section{Problem Statement}

For the estimated \textbf{4.8 billion} social media users worldwide---the majority under 30---recommendation algorithms operate invisibly. The logic that decides which post appears first, and which disappears entirely, is never explained. The result is well-documented: filter bubbles, epistemic polarisation, and an accelerating erosion of shared informational reality. 

Existing Media and Information Literacy (MIL) curricula predominantly address \emph{what} misinformation is, often failing to address the underlying mechanics of \emph{why} it spreads. The AI-driven mathematical, architectural, and attentional systems that amplify content regardless of its factual quality remain a black box to users. This problem is particularly urgent now, as AI-generated content exponentially increases the volume of online misinformation, making mere fact-checking insufficient without a foundational understanding of algorithmic amplification.

\section{Target Audience}

Our primary target audience consists of \textbf{Secondary students (ages 14--18)} and global youth learners. 

\textbf{Why this audience?} This age group is critical, as they are forming their digital identities and long-term social media habits. They are highly active online but often lack the critical framework to understand how recommendation algorithms shape their informational environment.

\textbf{How will we reach them?} Through direct partnerships with schools, UNESCO-affiliated workshops, and civil society youth organisations. The tool is built 100\% in English, requires zero installation (browser-based), and is optimized for low-bandwidth environments, specifically ensuring that \textbf{marginalized and underserved youth} with low-end devices can seamlessly participate.

\textbf{User Journey:} The student explores the feed. Once their feed falls below 35\% diversity, a trigger pauses the experience, showing them exactly how fast they entered an echo chamber. They play the Arena game to evaluate content, and use the Toolkit to pledge behavioural changes.

\textbf{Direct connection to MIL principles:} EchoBreaker moves beyond passive theory. By displaying the scoring engine, it teaches the \textbf{Algorithm} pillar. By re-ranking based on 2-second dwell times, it demonstrates the \textbf{Attention} economy. By providing the toolkit, it builds digital \textbf{Agency}.

\section{Innovation \& Creativity}

\begin{criteriabox}{Core Differentiator --- The Meta-Audit}
EchoBreaker uses \textbf{the same persuasive techniques it teaches students to resist}.
Glowing achievement badges, 3D card-flip animations, XP-style progress bars, and dopamine-reward unlocks are all deliberately present.

The reflection section explicitly reveals this contradiction:
\begin{quote}
\itshape
``Did you notice the glowing badges and 3D animations? We intentionally used \textbf{standard persuasive design (gamification)} to keep you engaged while teaching you how to resist it. How does it feel to recognise these emotional triggers working on you \emph{right now}?''
\end{quote}
\end{criteriabox}

This \textbf{Meta-Audit} is our central design innovation. The contradiction is the lesson. Technically, the live scoring transparency and dwell-time simulation using \texttt{IntersectionObserver} provide a realism rarely seen in educational tools, without requiring a massive backend infrastructure.

\section{Sustainability}

\textbf{Continuation:} The tool is open-source and requires zero active maintenance to keep running due to its static nature. The content pool is a simple JSON structure, allowing non-technical educators to add culturally relevant scenarios independently without touching code.

\textbf{Funding Model:} Given the \$0 monthly hosting overhead, the project is inherently self-sustaining on a technical level. Operational funding for educator workshops and global expansion will be sought through institutional grants, NGO partnerships, and potential UNESCO MIL network integration.

\textbf{Scalability:} Expanding to new regions only requires adding new scenario entries to the JSON content pool. The underlying engine remains exactly the same.

\textbf{UNESCO Values:} The project directly supports UNESCO's values of peace and freedom of expression by democratising the knowledge of how information is curated, ensuring that users are active participants in their digital lives rather than passive consumers.

By gamifying the learning experience and literally letting users "play their part" in breaking their own echo chambers, EchoBreaker embodies youth-led, action-oriented digital empowerment. We are turning a theoretical MIL concept into a tangible, experiential tool built by youth, for youth.

    \;+\; 0.10 \cdot R
\]
